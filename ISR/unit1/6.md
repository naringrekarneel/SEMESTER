# Discuss Different Similarity Measures Used in Information Retrieval (10 Marks)

## Introduction

In **Information Retrieval (IR)**, similarity measures are used to determine **how closely a document matches a user's query**. After representing documents and queries as vectors or sets of terms, a similarity measure calculates a score. Documents with **higher similarity scores** are ranked higher in the search results.

---

# Definition

A **similarity measure** is a mathematical technique used to calculate the degree of similarity between a query and a document. It helps IR systems retrieve the most relevant documents from a collection.

---

# Different Similarity Measures

## 1. Cosine Similarity

### Definition

Cosine Similarity measures the **angle between two vectors** (query and document). It is the most commonly used similarity measure in the **Vector Space Model**.

### Formula

$$
\text{Cosine Similarity}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q_iD_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}Q_i^2}\times\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_i^2}}
$$

Where:

* $Q_i$ = Weight of term $i$ in the query
* $D_i$ = Weight of term $i$ in the document

### Range

* **1** → Exactly similar
* **0** → Completely different

### Advantages

* Independent of document length.
* Works well with TF-IDF vectors.
* Most widely used in search engines.

### Example

Query: **"machine learning"**

* Document A contains both terms → High cosine similarity.
* Document B contains unrelated words → Low cosine similarity.

---

## 2. Jaccard Similarity

### Definition

Jaccard Similarity measures similarity based on the **common terms** between two sets.

### Formula

$$
J(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|}
$$

Where:

* $A$ = Query terms
* $B$ = Document terms

### Example

Query = {AI, ML, Python}

Document = {AI, Python, Java}

Intersection = {AI, Python} = 2

Union = {AI, ML, Python, Java} = 4

$$
J=\frac{2}{4}=0.5
$$

### Advantages

* Easy to compute.
* Suitable for set-based comparisons.
* Useful for duplicate document detection.

---

## 3. Dice Similarity Coefficient

### Definition

Dice Similarity gives more importance to the common terms between two sets.

### Formula

$$
Dice=\frac{2|A\cap B|}{|A|+|B|}
$$

### Example

Using the previous example:

$$
Dice=\frac{2\times2}{3+3}
=\frac{4}{6}
=0.667
$$

### Advantages

* Gives higher weight to overlapping terms.
* Effective for short documents and text matching.

---

## 4. Euclidean Distance

### Definition

Euclidean Distance measures the **straight-line distance** between two vectors.

### Formula

$$
Distance=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Q_i-D_i)^2}
$$

### Interpretation

* Smaller distance → Higher similarity.
* Larger distance → Lower similarity.

### Advantages

* Simple and intuitive.
* Suitable for numerical feature vectors.

### Limitation

* Sensitive to document length.
* Less commonly used than cosine similarity in IR.

---

## 5. Manhattan Distance

### Definition

Manhattan Distance calculates the **sum of absolute differences** between corresponding vector components.

### Formula

$$
Distance=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Q_i-D_i|
$$

### Advantages

* Easy to calculate.
* Useful for sparse data and high-dimensional vectors.

---

## 6. Pearson Correlation Coefficient

### Definition

Pearson Correlation measures the **linear relationship** between two vectors.

### Formula

$$
r=\frac{\sum(Q_i-\bar Q)(D_i-\bar D)}
{\sqrt{\sum(Q_i-\bar Q)^2}\sqrt{\sum(D_i-\bar D)^2}}
$$

### Range

* **+1** → Perfect positive correlation
* **0** → No correlation
* **−1** → Perfect negative correlation

### Applications

* Recommendation systems.
* User preference analysis.

---

## Comparison of Similarity Measures

| Similarity Measure  | Formula Basis          | Range    | Best Use                     |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------- |
| Cosine Similarity   | Angle between vectors  | 0 to 1   | TF-IDF, Vector Space Model   |
| Jaccard Similarity  | Set overlap            | 0 to 1   | Duplicate detection          |
| Dice Similarity     | Common elements        | 0 to 1   | Short text comparison        |
| Euclidean Distance  | Straight-line distance | 0 to ∞   | Numerical feature vectors    |
| Manhattan Distance  | Absolute differences   | 0 to ∞   | Sparse/high-dimensional data |
| Pearson Correlation | Linear correlation     | -1 to +1 | Recommendation systems       |

# Conclusion

Similarity measures are fundamental to Information Retrieval because they determine **how closely a document matches a user's query**. Among all measures, **Cosine Similarity** is the most widely used due to its effectiveness with TF-IDF vectors and its independence from document length. Other measures such as **Jaccard, Dice, Euclidean, Manhattan, and Pearson Correlation** are used depending on the nature of the data and the application.
