# Text Pre-processing in Information Retrieval

## 1. Introduction

**Text pre-processing** is the process of cleaning and transforming raw text into a suitable format before it is given to an Information Retrieval (IR) system.

The main goal is to **remove unnecessary variations and noise** so that searching becomes faster and more accurate.

The major steps are:

**Raw Text → Tokenization → Stop-word Removal → Stemming/Lemmatization → Processed Text**

---

# 2. Tokenization

### Definition

**Tokenization** is the process of breaking a text into smaller units called **tokens**, usually words or sentences.

### Example

Original text:

> "Information retrieval is very useful."

After tokenization:

```text
Information
retrieval
is
very
useful
```

Each word becomes an individual token.

### Why is it needed?

* Converts text into manageable units.
* Makes it easier to analyze individual words.
* Forms the basis for further processing.

### Example in IR

For the query:

> **"machine learning algorithms"**

Tokens are:

```text
machine | learning | algorithms
```

---

# 3. Stop-word Removal

### Definition

**Stop-word removal** eliminates very common words that usually carry little useful information for retrieval.

Examples:

```text
the, is, a, an, of, on, in, and, for, to
```

### Example

Original:

> "The machine is used for learning."

After stop-word removal:

```text
machine | used | learning
```

Words such as **"the", "is", "for"** are removed.

### Advantages

* Reduces the number of words to process.
* Reduces index size.
* Improves processing speed.
* Removes words that often have low discriminative value.

### Note

Stop-word removal is **not always necessary**. Some applications may need words such as *"not"* because removing them can change the meaning of a query.

---

# 4. Stemming

### Definition

**Stemming** reduces different forms of a word to a common **stem** by removing prefixes or suffixes.

It usually uses simple rules and may produce a word that is **not a valid dictionary word**.

### Example

```text
playing  → play
played   → play
plays    → play
```

Another example:

```text
connect
connected
connecting
connection

        ↓

connect
```

### Important point

A stem does **not always have to be a valid word**.

For example, some stemming algorithms may produce:

```text
studies → studi
```

Although **"studi"** is not an English word, it can still be useful for matching related terms.

### Advantages

* Simple and fast.
* Reduces vocabulary size.
* Helps match different forms of the same word.

---

# 5. Lemmatization

### Definition

**Lemmatization** converts a word into its proper **dictionary/base form**, called a **lemma**.

Unlike stemming, lemmatization considers the **meaning and grammatical form** of the word.

### Examples

```text
studies  → study
better   → good
running  → run
children → child
```

The resulting word is normally a valid dictionary word.

### Advantages

* Produces meaningful base words.
* More linguistically accurate than stemming.
* Useful when preserving the meaning of words is important.

### Disadvantage

It is generally **slower and more computationally expensive** than stemming because it requires more linguistic analysis.

---

# 6. Stemming vs Lemmatization

| Feature                 | Stemming          | Lemmatization                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Method**              | Uses simple rules | Uses linguistic/dictionary analysis |
| **Output**              | Stem              | Valid base/lemma                    |
| **Accuracy**            | Lower             | Higher                              |
| **Speed**               | Faster            | Slower                              |
| **Example**             | `studies → studi` | `studies → study`                   |
| **Meaning considered?** | Usually no        | Yes                                 |
| **Complexity**          | Simple            | More complex                        |

### Easy memory trick:

**Stemming = Cut the word**

**Lemmatization = Understand the word**

---

# 7. Complete Example

Consider the sentence:

> **"The students are studying different books."**

### Step 1: Tokenization

```text
The | students | are | studying | different | books
```

### Step 2: Stop-word Removal

Remove common words:

```text
students | studying | different | books
```

### Step 3: Stemming

Possible result:

```text
student | studi | differ | book
```

### Step 4: Lemmatization

Possible result:

```text
student | study | different | book
```

Thus, the raw sentence is transformed into a simpler representation that is easier for an IR system to search and compare.

---

# 8. Summary

```text
                RAW TEXT
                    ↓
              TOKENIZATION
                    ↓
          Individual words/tokens
                    ↓
           STOP-WORD REMOVAL
                    ↓
          Remove common words
                    ↓
        STEMMING / LEMMATIZATION
                    ↓
          Base/normalized words
                    ↓
          PROCESSED TEXT
                    ↓
           INFORMATION RETRIEVAL
```

### Exam-ready conclusion

**Text pre-processing is an essential step in an IR system because it reduces noise, decreases the size of the vocabulary, and improves search efficiency. Tokenization divides text into tokens, stop-word removal eliminates common words, stemming reduces words to their stems, and lemmatization converts words into their meaningful dictionary forms.**
