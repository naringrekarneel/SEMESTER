# Neural Text Representations and Word Embeddings

## 1. Introduction

Traditional text representation methods such as **Bag-of-Words** and **TF-IDF** represent words using sparse numerical vectors. However, they have a major limitation: they do not properly capture the **meaning and relationship between words**.

**Neural text representations** use neural networks to convert words, sentences, or documents into **dense numerical vectors called embeddings**.

For example:

```text
"king" → [0.21, -0.45, 0.73, ...]
"queen" → [0.19, -0.42, 0.71, ...]
```

Words with similar meanings tend to have similar vector representations.

---

# 2. What is a Word Embedding?

A **word embedding** is a dense vector representation of a word in a continuous numerical space.

Instead of representing a word as:

```text
cat → [0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0, ...]
```

an embedding represents it as:

```text
cat → [0.25, 0.71, -0.32, 0.18, ...]
```

The vector captures useful **semantic and syntactic relationships** learned from the text.

### Example

Words such as:

```text
car
vehicle
automobile
```

will generally have vectors that are close to one another.

---

# 3. Why Neural Text Representations?

Traditional representations:

* Produce very large vectors.
* Are often sparse.
* Treat words as independent symbols.
* Have difficulty capturing semantic relationships.

Neural embeddings:

* Produce dense vectors.
* Capture semantic relationships.
* Represent similar words using similar vectors.
* Can be used as input to neural networks.
* Improve many NLP and IR tasks.

---

# 4. Word2Vec

**Word2Vec** is a neural-network-based technique developed for learning word embeddings from large text collections.

It represents a word based on its **context**.

The central idea is:

> **Words appearing in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.**

Word2Vec has two main architectures:

### A. CBOW — Continuous Bag of Words

CBOW predicts the **target word from surrounding context words**.

Example:

```text
"The cat ___ on the mat"

Context → "The cat ... on the mat"
                    ↓
              Neural Network
                    ↓
              "sat"
```

So:

**Context → Target word**

---

### B. Skip-Gram

Skip-Gram does the opposite.

It uses a **target word to predict surrounding context words**.

Example:

```text
"The cat sat on the mat"

Target word → "cat"

          ↓
      Neural Network
          ↓
"The", "sat"
```

So:

**Target word → Context words**

---

## Word2Vec Architecture

```text
             Input Word
                  ↓
          ┌──────────────┐
          │ Neural       │
          │ Network      │
          └──────┬───────┘
                 ↓
           Word Embedding
                 ↓
        Context / Target
          Prediction
```

### Advantages

* Simple and efficient.
* Produces meaningful word vectors.
* Captures semantic relationships.
* Works well with large text collections.

### Limitation

A word generally gets **one fixed vector**, regardless of its context.

For example, the word **"bank"** has different meanings:

* river bank
* financial bank

Basic Word2Vec does not create separate contextual representations for these meanings.

---

# 5. GloVe

**GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation)** is another word-embedding technique.

Unlike Word2Vec, which learns mainly from **local context windows**, GloVe makes use of **global word co-occurrence statistics** across the entire corpus.

### Basic idea

If two words frequently occur in similar contexts, their vectors should be similar.

For example:

```text
                 Context
                    ↓
        ┌─────────────────────┐
        │ Word Co-occurrence  │
        │      Statistics     │
        └──────────┬──────────┘
                   ↓
             GloVe Model
                   ↓
           Word Embeddings
```

### Example

Suppose the corpus contains:

```text
"ice is cold"
"ice melts"
"steam is hot"
"steam rises"
```

GloVe examines how frequently words occur together and learns vectors based on these relationships.

### Advantages

* Uses global statistical information.
* Produces meaningful semantic representations.
* Efficient for large datasets.

### Limitation

Like basic Word2Vec, GloVe generally assigns **one vector per word**, so it does not naturally handle different meanings of the same word depending on context.

---

# 6. FastText

**FastText** was developed by Facebook AI Research.

The major difference is:

> **FastText represents a word using smaller character-level pieces called character n-grams.**

For example:

```text
"playing"
```

can be represented using character fragments such as:

```text
pla
lay
ayi
yin
ing
```

The exact subword construction depends on the implementation and boundary markers.

These subword representations are combined to create the word's embedding.

---

## Why is FastText Useful?

Consider a word that the model has never seen before:

```text
"unhappiness"
```

Even if the complete word is unseen, its smaller pieces may have been seen:

```text
un
happy
ness
```

Therefore, FastText can produce useful representations for **rare and unseen words** better than standard word-level methods.

### Advantages

* Handles rare words effectively.
* Can generate vectors for out-of-vocabulary words.
* Captures morphological information.
* Works well for morphologically rich languages.

### Limitation

* More computationally expensive because of subword representations.
* Requires more storage than simple word-level embeddings.

---

# 7. Word2Vec vs GloVe vs FastText

| Feature                | Word2Vec                       | GloVe                               | FastText                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Main idea**          | Learns from context prediction | Learns from global co-occurrence    | Learns using word + character n-grams |
| **Approach**           | Neural predictive model        | Matrix/co-occurrence-based approach | Subword-based neural embedding        |
| **Context**            | Local context                  | Global statistics                   | Context + subword information         |
| **Handles rare words** | Limited                        | Limited                             | **Better**                            |
| **OOV words**          | Poor                           | Poor                                | **Good**                              |
| **Morphology**         | Limited                        | Limited                             | **Strong**                            |
| **Main architectures** | CBOW, Skip-Gram                | GloVe objective                     | Subword-based                         |
| **Typical strength**   | Semantic relationships         | Global word relationships           | Rare/unseen words                     |

---

# 8. Simple Comparison

Think of the three methods like this:

### Word2Vec

> **"What words appear around this word?"**

### GloVe

> **"How often do words occur together across the whole corpus?"**

### FastText

> **"What does this word mean, and what smaller pieces make up this word?"**

# 9. Applications in Information Retrieval and NLP

Neural text representations and word embeddings are used in:

* Search engines
* Document similarity
* Semantic search
* Query expansion
* Text classification
* Sentiment analysis
* Machine translation
* Question answering
* Recommendation systems
* Named Entity Recognition

For example, a traditional keyword search may struggle with:

> **"automobile repair"**

and

> **"car servicing"**

because the exact words differ.

Embedding-based retrieval can recognize that **automobile ≈ car** and **repair ≈ servicing**, allowing more semantically relevant results.

# 11. Conclusion

**Neural text representations convert words and text into dense numerical vectors that capture semantic and syntactic relationships. Word2Vec learns embeddings using context prediction through CBOW and Skip-Gram, GloVe uses global word co-occurrence statistics, and FastText additionally uses character-level subwords. These embeddings are widely used in semantic search, document similarity, classification, translation, and other NLP and IR applications.**

### Exam memory trick

**Word2Vec → Context prediction**

**GloVe → Global co-occurrence**

**FastText → Character subwords**
