# Explain TF-IDF Weighting with Formula and Numerical Example (10 Marks)

## Introduction

In Information Retrieval (IR), not all words are equally important. Common words like **"the"**, **"is"**, and **"and"** appear in almost every document and are not useful for distinguishing documents. **TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency)** is a widely used term-weighting technique that measures how important a word is in a document relative to a collection of documents.

---

# Definition

**TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency)** is a statistical weighting method that assigns higher weights to terms that:

* Appear frequently in a particular document (**high TF**), and
* Appear in fewer documents across the collection (**high IDF**).

Thus, important words receive high weights, while common words receive low weights.

---

# Components of TF-IDF

## 1. Term Frequency (TF)

Term Frequency measures how often a term appears in a document.

### Formula

$$
TF(t,d)=\frac{\text{Number of times term }t\text{ appears in document }d}{\text{Total number of terms in document }d}
$$

### Interpretation

* Higher TF → More important within that document.
* Lower TF → Less important.

---

## 2. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)

IDF measures how rare or unique a term is across all documents.

### Formula

$$
IDF(t)=\log\left(\frac{N}{DF(t)}\right)
$$

Where:

* $N$ = Total number of documents
* $DF(t)$ = Number of documents containing term $t$

### Interpretation

* Rare terms → High IDF
* Common terms → Low IDF

---

## 3. TF-IDF Formula

The TF-IDF weight is calculated by multiplying TF and IDF.

### Formula

$$
TF\text{-}IDF(t,d)=TF(t,d)\times IDF(t)
$$

---

# Numerical Example

Suppose we have **5 documents**.

The term **"computer"**:

* Appears **4 times** in Document D1.
* Total words in D1 = **100**.
* Appears in **2 documents** out of 5.

### Step 1: Calculate TF

$$
TF=\frac{4}{100}=0.04
$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate IDF

$$
IDF=\log\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)
$$

Using base-10 logarithm:

$$
IDF=\log(2.5)\approx0.398
$$

---

### Step 3: Calculate TF-IDF

$$
TF\text{-}IDF=0.04\times0.398
$$

$$
TF\text{-}IDF\approx0.0159
$$

**Final TF-IDF Weight = 0.0159**

---

# Another Simple Example

| Term     | TF | DF |   IDF | TF-IDF |
| -------- | -: | -: | ----: | -----: |
| Computer |  5 |  2 | 0.398 |   1.99 |
| Data     |  4 |  5 |     0 |      0 |
| Neural   |  3 |  1 | 0.699 |  2.097 |

### Observation

* **Neural** has the highest TF-IDF because it is **rare**.
* **Data** appears in every document, so its IDF becomes **0**, making its TF-IDF also **0**.

---

# Working of TF-IDF

1. Count the frequency of each term in every document.
2. Compute the TF value.
3. Calculate the document frequency (DF) of each term.
4. Compute the IDF value.
5. Multiply TF and IDF to obtain the final weight.
6. Use these weights to rank documents according to their relevance.

---

# Advantages

* Simple and easy to compute.
* Highlights important keywords.
* Reduces the impact of common words.
* Improves document ranking.
* Widely used in search engines, document classification, and text mining.

---

# Disadvantages

* Does not consider the meaning (semantics) of words.
* Cannot handle synonyms and polysemy effectively.
* Ignores word order and context.
* Performs less effectively than modern embedding-based methods for semantic search.

---

# Applications

* Search engines
* Information Retrieval systems
* Document ranking
* Text classification
* Keyword extraction
* Recommendation systems

---

# Conclusion

TF-IDF is one of the most popular term-weighting techniques in Information Retrieval. It combines **Term Frequency (TF)** and **Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)** to identify important words in a document. Words that occur frequently in a document but rarely across the document collection receive the highest weights, making TF-IDF highly effective for document ranking and retrieval.
