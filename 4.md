# TF-IDF Weighting

## 1. Introduction

**TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency)** is a popular technique used in Information Retrieval to determine **how important a word is in a document within a collection of documents**.

The basic idea is:

> A word is important if it occurs **frequently in a particular document** but occurs **rarely across the entire document collection**.

For example, in a collection of documents, the word **"the"** appears everywhere, so it is not very useful. But a word like **"neural"** appearing frequently in one particular document can be more important.

---

# 2. Components of TF-IDF

TF-IDF has two components:

### 1. TF — Term Frequency

Measures **how frequently a term occurs in a document**.

A simple formula is:

[
TF(t,d)=\frac{\text{Number of times term }t\text{ occurs in document }d}
{\text{Total number of terms in document }d}
]

Where:

* (t) = term
* (d) = document

---

### 2. IDF — Inverse Document Frequency

Measures **how rare or important a term is across the document collection**.

[
IDF(t)=\log\left(\frac{N}{df(t)}\right)
]

Where:

* (N) = total number of documents
* (df(t)) = number of documents containing term (t)

If a word appears in many documents, its IDF is low.

If a word appears in very few documents, its IDF is high.

---

# 3. TF-IDF Formula

The complete formula is:

[
\boxed{TF\text{-}IDF(t,d)=TF(t,d)\times IDF(t)}
]

Therefore,

[
\boxed{
TF\text{-}IDF(t,d)
==================

\frac{f_{t,d}}{\text{Total terms in }d}
\times
\log\left(\frac{N}{df(t)}\right)
}
]

---

# 4. Numerical Example

Suppose we have **4 documents**:

```text
D1 = "data mining is useful"
D2 = "data mining techniques"
D3 = "data analysis techniques"
D4 = "machine learning techniques"
```

We want to calculate the **TF-IDF of the word "mining" in D1**.

---

### Step 1: Calculate TF

In D1:

```text
data mining is useful
```

Total number of terms = **4**

Number of times **mining** occurs = **1**

Therefore:

[
TF(\text{mining},D1)=\frac{1}{4}=0.25
]

---

### Step 2: Calculate DF

The word **"mining"** occurs in:

```text
D1 ✓
D2 ✓
D3 ✗
D4 ✗
```

Therefore:

[
df(\text{mining})=2
]

Total documents:

[
N=4
]

---

### Step 3: Calculate IDF

Using:

[
IDF(t)=\log_{10}\left(\frac{N}{df(t)}\right)
]

[
IDF(\text{mining})
==================

\log_{10}\left(\frac{4}{2}\right)
]

[
=\log_{10}(2)
]

[
\approx 0.301
]

---

### Step 4: Calculate TF-IDF

[
TF\text{-}IDF
=============

TF\times IDF
]

[
=0.25\times0.301
]

[
\boxed{TF\text{-}IDF\approx0.0753}
]

Therefore, the TF-IDF weight of **"mining" in D1 is approximately 0.0753**.

---

# 5. Another Quick Example

Suppose the word **"data"** occurs in **all 4 documents**.

[
df(\text{data})=4
]

Then:

[
IDF=\log_{10}\left(\frac{4}{4}\right)
]

[
=\log_{10}(1)=0
]

Therefore:

[
TF\text{-}IDF=TF\times0=0
]

This shows an important property of TF-IDF:

> **Very common words receive a low or zero weight because they provide little information for distinguishing documents.**

---

# 6. Interpretation of TF-IDF

| Situation                                                    | TF-IDF Weight                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Term appears frequently in one document and rarely elsewhere | **High**                     |
| Term appears rarely in a document                            | **Low**                      |
| Term appears in almost every document                        | **Very low**                 |
| Term appears in every document                               | **0** with the basic formula |

So:

[
\boxed{\text{High TF-IDF} \Rightarrow \text{Term is important for that document}}
]

---

# 7. Applications

TF-IDF is used in:

* Search engines
* Document ranking
* Information Retrieval
* Document similarity
* Text classification
* Keyword extraction
* Search query matching

---

## 8. Exam Summary

**TF-IDF combines Term Frequency (TF) and Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) to measure the importance of a term in a document. TF measures how frequently a term occurs in a document, while IDF reduces the weight of terms appearing in many documents. The final weight is calculated as (TF-IDF = TF \times IDF). A high TF-IDF value indicates that the term is relatively important and distinctive for that document.**
