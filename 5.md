# Probabilistic Index Term Weighting

## 1. Introduction

**Probabilistic index term weighting** assigns a weight to a term based on the **probability that the term is useful for retrieving relevant documents**.

It is based on the **Probabilistic Information Retrieval (IR) model**, where the system tries to estimate:

> **How likely is this document to be relevant to the user's query?**

Unlike TF-IDF, which mainly uses term frequency and document frequency, probabilistic weighting considers the **probability of a term occurring in relevant and non-relevant documents**.

---

# 2. Basic Idea

Suppose the user searches:

> **"machine learning"**

There are many documents in the collection.

The system tries to separate them into:

```text
Documents
     │
     ├── Relevant documents
     │
     └── Non-relevant documents
```

If a term appears frequently in relevant documents but rarely in non-relevant documents, it should receive a **high weight**.

For example:

```text
Term = "machine"

Relevant documents    → appears frequently
Non-relevant documents → appears rarely

                ↓

        High importance
```

---

# 3. Probabilistic Weight Formula

A common probabilistic term-weighting idea is based on the **odds ratio**:

[
w_t =
\log
\left(
\frac{P(t|R)/(1-P(t|R))}
{P(t|\overline{R})/(1-P(t|\overline{R}))}
\right)
]

Where:

* (t) = term
* (R) = set of relevant documents
* (\overline{R}) = set of non-relevant documents
* (P(t|R)) = probability that term (t) occurs in a relevant document
* (P(t|\overline{R})) = probability that term (t) occurs in a non-relevant document

### Interpretation

If:

[
P(t|R) > P(t|\overline{R})
]

then the term is more strongly associated with relevant documents and gets a **higher positive weight**.

---

# 4. Example

Suppose we have **100 relevant documents** and **900 non-relevant documents**.

Consider the term **"neural"**.

Assume:

* "neural" occurs in 60 relevant documents.
* "neural" occurs in 90 non-relevant documents.

Then:

[
P(t|R)=\frac{60}{100}=0.6
]

[
P(t|\overline R)=\frac{90}{900}=0.1
]

The term occurs much more frequently in relevant documents than in non-relevant documents.

Therefore, **"neural" receives a high positive weight**.

The important idea is more significant than the exact calculation:

> **The more strongly a term distinguishes relevant documents from non-relevant documents, the higher its probabilistic weight.**

---

# 5. TF-IDF vs Probabilistic Index Term Weighting

| Feature                    | TF-IDF                                               | Probabilistic Weighting                                 |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Basic idea**             | Measures term importance using frequency and rarity. | Measures how strongly a term indicates relevance.       |
| **Main factors**           | TF and IDF.                                          | Probability of term in relevant/non-relevant documents. |
| **Relevance information**  | Usually does not require relevance information.      | Can use relevance information.                          |
| **Formula basis**          | (TF \times IDF)                                      | Probability/odds-based                                  |
| **Learning from feedback** | Not inherently designed for it.                      | Can incorporate relevance feedback.                     |
| **Main goal**              | Identify important/distinctive terms.                | Estimate document relevance.                            |
| **Complexity**             | Simple                                               | More complex                                            |
| **Typical use**            | Basic search and text representation.                | Probabilistic IR and relevance-based ranking.           |

---

# 6. Key Difference with Example

Suppose the term **"python"** occurs in many documents.

### TF-IDF asks:

> **"How frequently does Python occur in this document, and how rare is it across the collection?"**

### Probabilistic weighting asks:

> **"How strongly does the presence of Python indicate that this document is relevant to the user's query?"**

That's the core difference.

---

# 7. Advantages of Probabilistic Weighting

1. **Directly considers relevance.**
2. Can distinguish terms appearing in relevant vs. non-relevant documents.
3. Can improve ranking using **relevance feedback**.
4. Provides a probabilistic interpretation of document relevance.
5. Forms the foundation for important probabilistic retrieval models such as **BM25**.

---

# 8. Limitations

1. Requires estimates of probabilities.
2. Initial relevance information may not be available.
3. More computationally complex than basic TF-IDF.
4. Probability estimates can be inaccurate when the collection is small.

---

# 9. Exam-Friendly Summary

**Probabilistic index term weighting assigns weights to terms according to their ability to distinguish relevant documents from non-relevant documents. It uses probabilities such as (P(t|R)) and (P(t|\overline R)) to estimate the importance of a term. TF-IDF mainly measures term frequency and rarity, whereas probabilistic weighting focuses on the probability of relevance. Probabilistic methods can also incorporate relevance feedback and form the basis of models such as BM25.**

### Easy memory trick:

**TF-IDF → "How important is this word?"**

**Probabilistic weighting → "How strongly does this word indicate relevance?"**
