# Vanishing and Exploding Gradients & Weight Initialization — 10 Marks

## 1. Introduction

In Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), training is typically performed using **Gradient Descent** and **Backpropagation**. The optimization algorithm computes the gradient of the loss function $L$ with respect to each weight $W$ and bias $b$ to update the parameters:

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_t}$$

As networks grow deeper, gradients computed via the backpropagation chain rule must travel across tens or hundreds of hidden layers from the output back to the input. During this backward flow, gradients can either:
1. **Vanish exponentially toward zero** ($\to 0$), freezing early layers.
2. **Explode exponentially toward infinity** ($\to \infty$), causing chaotic parameter updates or arithmetic overflow (`NaN`).

**Proper weight initialization** plays a critical role in stabilizing signal variance across forward activations and backward gradients, preventing both phenomena at the start of training.

---

## 2. Vanishing Gradient Problem

```text
Output Layer  ──►  Layer L-1  ──►  ...  ──►  Layer 2  ──►  Layer 1 (Input)
  [Gradients]       (× 0.25)                (× 0.25)       Gradient ≈ 0 (Vanished!)
```

### Definition
The **vanishing gradient problem** occurs when the partial derivatives of the loss with respect to early layer parameters decay exponentially toward zero as they propagate backward through a deep architecture.

### ELI5 (Explain Like I'm 5)
Imagine passing a message down a chain of 20 people. If each person whispers the message at only half the volume of the person before them, by the time it reaches person #1, the sound is completely inaudible. The first person receives no instruction and cannot learn.

### Mathematical Root Cause
For a network with $L$ layers where $z^{[l]} = W^{[l]} a^{[l-1]} + b^{[l]}$ and $a^{[l]} = \sigma(z^{[l]})$, the chain rule yields:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{[1]}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{[L]}} \cdot \left( \prod_{k=2}^{L} \frac{\partial a^{[k]}}{\partial z^{[k]}} W^{[k]} \right) \cdot \frac{\partial z^{[1]}}{\partial W^{[1]}}$$

* When using saturating activation functions like **Sigmoid**:
  $$\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z)) \le 0.25$$
* Even if weights are initialized around $1$, the derivative term contributes at most $0.25$ per layer.
* Over a 10-layer path, the gradient shrinks by at least:
  $$(0.25)^{10} \approx 9.5 \times 10^{-7}$$
* When combined with small initial weights ($\|W\| < 1$), the gradient vanishes almost instantaneously.

### Causes
1. **Repeated Jacobian Multiplication:** Multiplying matrices with spectral radius $\rho < 1$ across $L$ layers.
2. **Saturating Activations:** Sigmoid ($\max \sigma' = 0.25$) and Tanh ($\max \tanh' = 1.0$, but quickly saturates to $0$ for large $|z|$).
3. **Overly Small Weight Initialization:** Initializing weights too close to zero ($W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01)$).

### Effects
* Early layers update at a negligible pace, effectively remaining random filters.
* Deep networks fail to outperform shallow baselines because feature representation learning is crippled.
* Drastically slows down convergence or stops optimization completely.

## 3. Exploding Gradient Problem

```text
Output Layer  ──►  Layer L-1  ──►  ...  ──►  Layer 2  ──►  Layer 1 (Input)
  [Gradients]        (× 2.0)                 (× 2.0)       Gradient ≈ 1024 (Exploded!)
```

### Definition
The **exploding gradient problem** occurs when the gradients accumulate exponentially during backpropagation, leading to massive parameter updates that destabilize training.

### ELI5 (Explain Like I'm 5)
Imagine a small snowball rolling down a mountain that doubles in size with every rotation. By the time it hits the bottom, it has grown into a massive avalanche that crushes everything in its path.

### Mathematical Root Cause
Referring back to the chain rule product:

$$\prod_{k=2}^{L} \left( \sigma'(z^{[k]}) W^{[k]} \right)$$

If the eigenvalues of the weight matrices exceed $1$ ($\|W\| > 1$) and activation derivatives do not sufficiently compress the values (e.g., in deep linear networks or unbounded ReLU regions where $\sigma'(z) = 1$ for $z > 0$):
* A product of factors greater than $1$ (e.g., $1.5$ or $2.0$) grows exponentially:
  $$(2.0)^{10} = 1024, \quad (2.0)^{20} \approx 1.05 \times 10^6$$
* The gradient magnitude explodes uncontrollably.

### Causes
1. **Excessively Large Initial Weights:** Drawing weights from wide distributions without accounting for layer fan-in.
2. **Deep Architecture Depth:** Compounding effects over dozens or hundreds of layers.
3. **Unbounded Activations Without Normalization:** In deep networks using linear or unbounded functions without normalization layers.

### Effects
* **Numerical Instability:** Floating-point overflow results in `Inf` or `NaN` losses.
* **Catastrophic Parameter Oscillation:** Weights take huge steps across the loss landscape, jumping completely out of optimal basins.
* Training diverges immediately within the first few mini-batches.

---

## 4. How Proper Weight Initialization Mitigates These Issues

The core objective of weight initialization is to **preserve activation variance during the forward pass** and **preserve gradient variance during the backward pass**:

$$\operatorname{Var}(a^{[l]}) = \operatorname{Var}(a^{[l-1]}) \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{[l-1]}}\right) = \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{[l]}}\right)$$

If variance neither shrinks to zero nor blows up to infinity across layers, both vanishing and exploding gradients are naturally prevented.

```text
                     WEIGHT INITIALIZATION SCHEMES
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                   ▼
[Xavier / Glorot Init]                              [He / Kaiming Init]
• Designed for: Sigmoid, Tanh                       • Designed for: ReLU, Leaky ReLU
• Compensates for: Symmetric linear regions         • Compensates for: 50% dead zero-regions
• Variance: 2 / (fan_in + fan_out)                  • Variance: 2 / fan_in
```

---

### A. Xavier (Glorot) Initialization (for Sigmoid / Tanh)

Proposed by Xavier Glorot and Yoshua Bengio (2010), this scheme assumes linear/symmetric activations operating around zero ($\tanh$).

For a linear combination $z_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n_{in}} W_{ij} x_j$ where $W$ and $x$ are zero-mean independent variables:

$$\operatorname{Var}(z_i) = n_{in} \cdot \operatorname{Var}(W) \cdot \operatorname{Var}(x)$$

To maintain $\operatorname{Var}(z_i) = \operatorname{Var}(x)$, we require:

$$\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{1}{n_{in}}$$

Similarly, to preserve gradient variance during the backward pass:

$$\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{1}{n_{out}}$$

Taking the harmonic mean of both conditions yields the **Glorot (Xavier) Variance**:

$$\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{in} + n_{out}}$$

#### Implementation Formulas:
* **Normal Distribution:**
  $$W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \, \frac{2}{n_{in} + n_{out}}\right)$$
* **Uniform Distribution:** Since $\operatorname{Var}(U[-a, a]) = \frac{a^2}{3}$, setting $\frac{a^2}{3} = \frac{2}{n_{in} + n_{out}}$ gives:
  $$W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{in} + n_{out}}}, \, +\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{in} + n_{out}}}\right)$$

---

### B. He (Kaiming) Initialization (for ReLU / Leaky ReLU)

Proposed by Kaiming He et al. (2015), this method solves the limitation of Xavier initialization when applied to **ReLU activations** ($\max(0, z)$).

* Because ReLU zeroes out all negative values ($z < 0$), approximately **half of the neurons are inactive ($50\%$)** on any forward pass.
* Consequently, ReLU cuts the variance of the activations in half:
  $$\operatorname{Var}(a) = \frac{1}{2} n_{in} \operatorname{Var}(W) \operatorname{Var}(x)$$
* If Xavier initialization is used with ReLU, activation variance is halved at every single layer ($0.5^L \to 0$), causing the signal to vanish.
* To counteract this $50\%$ drop, He initialization doubles the variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{in}}$$

#### Implementation Formulas:
* **Normal Distribution:**
  $$W \sim \mathcal{N}\left(0, \, \frac{2}{n_{in}}\right) \implies \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{in}}}$$
* **Uniform Distribution:**
  $$W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{in}}}, \, +\sqrt{\frac{6}{n_{in}}}\right)$$

---

### C. LeCun Initialization (for SELU / Linear)

Proposed by Yann LeCun (1998):
$$\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{1}{n_{in}}$$
Used extensively with Scaled Exponential Linear Units (SELU) to induce self-normalizing properties across layers.

## 5. Complementary Techniques to Prevent Gradient Problems

While weight initialization stabilizes the network at step $t=0$, auxiliary architectural techniques ensure stability throughout training:

| Technique | Problem Addressed | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ReLU / Leaky ReLU** | Vanishing Gradients | Derivative is $1.0$ for $z > 0$, preventing activation derivative shrinkage. |
| **Residual Connections (ResNet)** | Vanishing Gradients | Skip connections introduce identity mapping: $\frac{\partial x_{l+1}}{\partial x_l} = 1 + \frac{\partial F}{\partial x_l}$, allowing gradients to bypass multiplicative layers. |
| **Gradient Clipping** | Exploding Gradients | Caps gradient vector norm: if $\|g\|_2 > c$, then $g \leftarrow \frac{c}{\|g\|_2} g$. |
| **Batch Normalization (BN)** | Both Vanishing & Exploding | Normalizes inputs to each layer to $\mu=0, \sigma^2=1$, preventing activations from drifting into saturated regimes. |

## 6. Summary Comparison Table

| Property | Vanishing Gradients | Exploding Gradients |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gradient Magnitude** | $\|\nabla L\| \to 0$ exponentially | $\|\nabla L\| \to \infty$ exponentially |
| **Primary Symptom** | Training freezes; early layers do not learn | Loss diverges to `NaN`/`Inf`; weights oscillate |
| **Vulnerable Functions** | Sigmoid ($\max \sigma' = 0.25$), Tanh | Linear, Unbounded activations with large weights |
| **Initialization Solution** | Xavier ($Var = \frac{2}{n_{in}+n_{out}}$), He ($Var = \frac{2}{n_{in}}$) | He/Xavier preventing overly large random weights |
| **Auxiliary Solution** | ReLU, Skip connections (ResNets), BatchNorm | Gradient Clipping, Weight Decay ($L_2$), Lower LR |

---

## 7. Conclusion

The vanishing and exploding gradient problems represent fundamental numerical hurdles in training deep architectures caused by repeated Jacobian matrix multiplications across depth. **Proper weight initialization (Xavier for Sigmoid/Tanh, He for ReLU)** directly prevents these issues at initialization by calibrating parameter variance to match layer fan-in and fan-out. Paired with non-saturating activations (ReLU), residual connections, and gradient clipping, modern deep networks can train stably across hundreds of layers.

## Quick Revision for Exams

* **Vanishing Gradients:** Factor $< 1$ repeatedly multiplied $\implies$ gradient goes to $0$ $\implies$ Sigmoid is the main culprit.
* **Exploding Gradients:** Factor $> 1$ repeatedly multiplied $\implies$ gradient goes to $\infty$ $\implies$ `NaN` loss $\implies$ solved by Gradient Clipping.
* **Xavier (Glorot):** $\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{in} + n_{out}}$ (best for Tanh & Sigmoid).
* **He (Kaiming):** $\operatorname{Var}(W) = \frac{2}{n_{in}}$ (best for ReLU because half the activations are zeroed).

### 🧠 One-line memory trick
> **Xavier balances both sides for Tanh; He doubles the input variance for ReLU!**
