# Topic 7: Applications of Agentic AI in Industry

This is the **final topic in your syllabus**.

The big idea is simple:

> Traditional AI often **predicts or generates**.
> Agentic AI can **decide, plan, use tools, and execute tasks**.

That's why agents are useful across almost every industry.

---

# 1. Agentic AI in Healthcare

### What can an agent do?

A healthcare agent can:

* collect patient information
* summarize medical records
* schedule appointments
* analyze test reports
* monitor patients
* remind patients about medications
* assist doctors with decision-making

### Example

A patient says:

> "I need an appointment with a cardiologist next week."

The agent can:

```text
Understand request
↓
Check patient preferences
↓
Find available doctors
↓
Compare timings
↓
Book appointment
↓
Send confirmation
```

The important part is that the agent performs the **multi-step process**.

### Benefits

* Saves staff time
* Faster administrative work
* Personalized assistance
* 24/7 availability

### Limitation

High-risk medical decisions generally require **human oversight**.

# 13. Why Industry Loves Agentic AI

The main advantage is **task automation**.

Traditional automation:

```text
Input
↓
Fixed rules
↓
Output
```

Agentic automation:

```text
Goal
↓
Understand
↓
Plan
↓
Use tools
↓
Act
↓
Observe
↓
Adapt
```

This allows systems to deal with situations that aren't perfectly predictable.

# 15. Challenges and Risks

This is **very important for modern AI interviews**.

Agentic AI isn't magic. More autonomy means more things can go wrong.

### 1. Hallucination

An agent may reason from incorrect information.

### 2. Incorrect Actions

A wrong decision can produce real-world consequences.

### 3. Security

Agents with powerful tools can become dangerous if compromised.

### 4. Privacy

Agents may access sensitive user or company information.

### 5. Lack of Explainability

It may be difficult to understand why an agent chose an action.

### 6. Cost

Long multi-step reasoning and tool calls can be expensive.

### 7. Human Oversight

High-impact actions often need approval.

---

# 16. Human-in-the-Loop

A practical solution is:

```text
Agent
 ↓
Analyze
 ↓
Plan
 ↓
High-risk action?
 ↓
YES
 ↓
Human Approval
 ↓
Execute
```

Example:

> Agent finds a ₹2,00,000 transaction that appears suspicious.

Instead of automatically freezing the account, it can ask a human analyst to approve the action.

This gives us:

> **Autonomy where useful + human control where necessary.**

# 17. Worked Example: Agentic AI in an Online Store

Suppose a customer says:

> "My order is late. Fix it."

A traditional chatbot might reply:

> "Your order is delayed."

An agentic system could:

```text
Understand complaint
        ↓
Retrieve order details
        ↓
Check shipment tracking
        ↓
Identify cause of delay
        ↓
Check available solutions
        ↓
Choose replacement/refund/re-delivery
        ↓
Execute action
        ↓
Verify success
        ↓
Notify customer
```

That's the difference between **answering** and **acting**.

# 18. Industry-Wide Pattern

Almost all agentic applications follow the same pattern:

$$
\text{Goal} \rightarrow \text{Observe} \rightarrow \text{Reason} \rightarrow \text{Plan} \rightarrow \text{Act} \rightarrow \text{Feedback}
$$

And then:

$$
\text{Feedback} \rightarrow \text{Replan}
$$

This connects directly back to Topics 1–6.
